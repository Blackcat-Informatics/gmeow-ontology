# Standpoint — alignment & projection reference

The alignment and projection companion to the [standpoint doctrine](../../../docs/standpoints.md).
Everything here is **authored once** in `mapping-dsl/` and generated by
`gmeow compile-mappings` (Principle 4); nothing in `mappings/`, `projections/`, or
`queries/projections/` is hand-edited.

## Terms

| Term | Kind | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `gmeow:accordingTo` | `owl:AnnotationProperty` | the standpoint axis — *according to whom* a statement holds |
| `gmeow:standpointModality` | `owl:AnnotationProperty` | the belief value: `unequivocal` (□/true), `probable`, `conceivable` (◊/possible), `refuted` (□¬/false) — spans Standpoint-Logic *and* CRMinf belief values |
| `gmeow:Standpoint` | class (`logic:Kind`) | a named perspective/frame (often a bare `gmeow:Agent` instead) |
| `gmeow:sharpens` | transitive object property | the standpoint ⊆ / precisification order |
| `gmeow:madeWithinContextOf` | object property (no characteristic) | relates a standpoint that exists for reading works to the frame whose context its readings were made within — never a sharpening, so the subject does not become a case of the object |
| `gmeow:moreEntrenchedThan` | object property (irreflexive) | a ranker's dated, partial ordering of frames by entrenchment — never derived from `sharpens`, never closed across rankers |
| `gmeow:universalStandpoint` | individual | the explicit-universal top of the `sharpens` poset `*` |
| `gmeow:unspecifiedStandpoint` | individual | the default-of-silence frame for unindexed claims (unspecified, not universal) |
| `gmeow:StandpointTenure` | class (`logic:Situation`) | reified standpoint-time (a position held over an interval) |
| `gmeow:Scenario` | class (`logic:Kind`) | a hypothesized situation under consideration — a context that entertains a supposition without asserting it; one of the distinct typed contexts of the context algebra, distinct from `logic:PossibleWorld` and `gmeow:Standpoint` |
| `gmeow:State` | class (`logic:Kind`) | one state of affairs along a history — the position on a `logic:Path` that a `logic:Situation` realizes; succession between States is the typed relation `logic:temporallySucceeds`, distinct from `logic:Situation` (the reified state-of-affairs snapshot) |

## Term anchors

### gmeow:Standpoint · gmeow:sharpens

A named perspective/frame within which claims are held true — a state's official
position, a community's usage, an editorial point of view — minted only when the
frame needs its own identity (often a bare `gmeow:Agent` suffices). Standpoints
form a poset under `sharpens` (transitive, the standpoint ⊆ refinement relation),
topped by `universalStandpoint`.

### gmeow:madeWithinContextOf

Relates a standpoint that exists for reading works to the frame whose context its
readings were made within. Two such standpoints may share a context without either
being a sharper case of the other, so it is not a subproperty of `sharpens` and entails
no `sharpens` edge. It is not transitive, symmetric or irreflexive: chaining would
rebuild a refinement-like order, the subject is the reader and the object the frame,
and a standpoint reading within its own context is a legitimate degenerate case. It is
not for a person who holds claims about other things as well; use `accordingTo` there.

### gmeow:moreEntrenchedThan

A strict partial order between standpoints that exists only as dated claims attributed
to the party who ranks them: every edge needs a reifier annotated with
`gmeow:accordingTo` (the ranker) and `gmeow:validFrom` (the instant from which the
ranking holds). A missing claim means the two frames are incomparable, never equal and
never lower. It is transitive within one ranker's live claims, which a canonical check
requires to be closed; it is not declared transitive or asymmetric globally, because that
would chain different rankers' claims into a ranking nobody made and would reject two
rankers who rank a pair in opposite directions (Principle 9). It is never derived from `sharpens`,
and it is a presentation aid, not a confidence or a truth value; the top of a ranker's
order is not a primary or preferred frame.

What the canonical checks enforce:

- `gmeow:moreEntrenchedThanIrreflexivity`: no standpoint is more entrenched than itself.
- `gmeow:moreEntrenchedThanAttributionConstraint`: an edge without a reifier carrying both
  `gmeow:accordingTo` and `gmeow:validFrom` is rejected.
- `gmeow:moreEntrenchedThanRankerOrderConstraint`: one ranker may not hold a pair in both
  directions at once. Two rankers may. The pair is exempt when one claim is linked to
  the other by `gmeow:succeedsClaim` or `gmeow:correctsClaim`, or when either claim is
  retracted with `gmeow:retractsClaim`.
- `gmeow:moreEntrenchedThanRankerClosureConstraint`: a ranker who holds a live claim
  ranking a over b and a live claim ranking b over c must also hold a live claim ranking
  a over c. A claim is live when it is the object of no `gmeow:succeedsClaim`, of no
  `gmeow:retractsClaim`, and of no `gmeow:correctsClaim` statement whose reifier names
  the same ranker with `gmeow:accordingTo`. The ranker is read from the annotation on the
  correction statement, not from the correcting claim: a correction statement that names
  another party leaves the claim live, whichever claim it is made from, because a
  correction does not supersede or suppress the claim it corrects. A premise that is
  superseded, retracted or corrected by a statement that names its own ranker does not
  oblige, and the concluding claim must itself be live.

Together these checks reject a cycle of any length among one ranker's live claims: the
closure forces a live claim from the first standpoint of the cycle to its last, and that
claim meets the claim closing the cycle as a reversed pair. `logic:transitiveReach` is not
used for this, because it follows one fixed property over asserted edges and cannot be
restricted to the reifiers that name one ranker.

What is not enforced: the checks read the record as it stands and compare no dates, so
they do not judge the order at an evaluation instant. A cycle among the claims selected
at an instant `t` below (for example among claims that were all in force before one of
them was superseded) is reported by the consumer. The checks apply to a claim whose
first standpoint is typed `gmeow:Standpoint`. Only the ranker's own acts count: the pair
check exempts two claims of one ranker when either succeeds or corrects the other through
a statement whose annotation names that ranker, or when an activity associated with that
ranker retracts either. The closure check counts a succession, a correction or a
retraction in the same way. A succession, correction or retraction by another party is
that party's statement about the ranking; it exempts nothing and ends no claim's
liveness.

A ranker who changes or withdraws a ranking keeps the earlier claim and uses the claim
lifecycle relations on the reifier of the ranking statement: `gmeow:succeedsClaim` for a
reversal or replacement (the earlier claim stays valid for its earlier interval),
`gmeow:correctsClaim` for a ranking made in error, and `gmeow:retractsClaim` for a
withdrawal, each annotated with the same ranker and an effective time. There is no
separate end instant for a ranking. Name the reifier of each ranking statement so these
relations can reach it; the constraints read only the ranker's own succession, correction
and retraction, so name the ranker on each annotation and on the retracting activity with
`gmeow:wasAssociatedWith`. A ranker's own correction of a ranking looks like this, with the ranker on
the annotation of the correction statement:

```turtle
ex:reviewedRanking gmeow:correctsClaim ex:mistakenRanking
    {| gmeow:accordingTo ex:editorialBoard ;
       gmeow:validFrom "2026-05-01T00:00:00Z"^^xsd:dateTime |} .
```

Which claims enter one ranker's order is fixed by a stated evaluation instant `t`. A
claim participates when it names the ranker with `gmeow:accordingTo` and carries a
`gmeow:validFrom` not later than `t`. Two uses differ on suppression:

- A displayed order leaves out any claim suppressed with `gmeow:displayable false`, since
  suppression is how superseded and withdrawn records leave display (Principle 10); it
  also leaves out a claim corrected by a correction statement that names the ranker with
  `gmeow:accordingTo`, a claim whose retraction has taken effect by `t`, and a claim
  whose successor has taken effect by `t`.
- A historical order at `t` gives the ranker's present account of what it ranked at
  `t`: it is read from the record as it now stands, so a correction the ranker makes
  later changes the account of earlier instants. It ignores
  `gmeow:displayable`, which is a display policy and not a truth judgment. It keeps a
  superseded claim for every `t` before its successor took effect and leaves it out from
  that instant on. It leaves out at every `t` a claim corrected by a correction statement
  that names the ranker, because the ranker then rejects the earlier validity of its own
  claim. A correction statement that names another party removes the claim from neither
  order: a correction does not supersede or suppress the claim it corrects. The corrected
  claim itself stays in the record either way. It keeps a retracted claim for every `t`
  before its retraction took effect and leaves it out from that instant on, because a
  retraction withdraws endorsement from then on and does not erase that the claim was
  made. A lifecycle relation takes effect at the earliest `gmeow:validFrom` on its own
  statement; one that carries no such date counts as in effect at every `t`.

In both uses the consumer closes the selected claims transitively, per ranker, and leaves
any pair still unordered incomparable. If the selected claims contain a cycle, the
ranker's ranking is inconsistent at `t`; a consumer reports it and does not pick a side.
Every case above is decided by the graph, the ranker, `t` and the use, so two consumers
given the same four select the same claims and derive the same order.

### gmeow:correctsClaim

Correction and suppression are two different acts. "It is blue" answered by "No, it is
red, you are wrong, I checked" does not erase the evidence of blue. Two patterns cover
the cases, and neither one touches the claim it is about.

| Case | Pattern | What is recorded |
|---|---|---|
| Two parties simply disagree; nothing is corrected. | A `gmeow:ClaimEvaluation` that `gmeow:evaluates` the claim | The evaluator's standpoint (`gmeow:accordingTo`) and the verdict it `gmeow:expresses`. Evaluations by other standpoints coexist. |
| A standpoint states, on a codified basis such as a checked record or a rule that decides between the two claims, that another claim is wrong. | `gmeow:correctsClaim` from the correcting claim to the corrected claim | The correcting standpoint (`gmeow:accordingTo`) and the effective time (`gmeow:validFrom`) on the relation. |

`gmeow:correctsClaim` is not a sub-property of `gmeow:supersedes`. A correction derives no
`gmeow:supersedes` and no `gmeow:supersededBy`, and it gives no reason to set
`gmeow:displayable false`: the corrected claim is retained unchanged and stays
retrievable beside its correction. No data sets `gmeow:displayable false` because a
claim is disputed, corrected or evaluated; what a particular reader sees of a disputed
claim is decided at projection. Principle 10 names the cases that do prompt suppression.
A claimed state that changed is `gmeow:succeedsClaim`, and a standpoint withdrawing its
own claim is `gmeow:retractsClaim`; neither is a dispute.

The suppression warning on a standpoint tenure (`gmeow:WithdrawnStandpointSuppressionConstraint`)
concerns a holder withdrawing its own position: it fires only for a tenure whose interval has
ended and whose held claim (`gmeow:standpointClaim`) is retracted by an activity that
`gmeow:wasAssociatedWith` the claim's own `gmeow:vantage`, which is the tenure's standpoint.
The retraction is read on the claim, the canonical encoding; a retraction with no stated
actor, or by another party, does not fire it. A tenure whose interval merely ended is not
reported, and a correction or dispute by another party never prompts a display flag.

```turtle
# Plain disagreement: the neighbour doubts the report and corrects nothing.
ex:neighbourDoubt a gmeow:ClaimEvaluation ;
    gmeow:evaluates   ex:blueClaim ;
    gmeow:accordingTo ex:neighbourView ;
    gmeow:expresses   ex:blueClaimUnwarranted .

# Correction: the inspector checked the paint record and says the report is wrong.
ex:redClaim gmeow:correctsClaim ex:blueClaim
    {| gmeow:accordingTo ex:inspectorView ;
       gmeow:validFrom "2026-04-02T09:00:00Z"^^xsd:dateTime |} .
```

The complete example is `examples/correction-and-disagreement.ttl`. Its competency
question in `tests/competency.ttl` asks for the claims of the inspector's correction and
receives both `ex:blueClaim` and `ex:redClaim`.

### gmeow:StandpointModality · gmeow:claimModality

The belief value a standpoint assigns a proposition — the closed vocabulary
spanning Standpoint-Logic □/◊ and the CRMinf values plus the Frankfurt bullshit
modality (`unequivocal`, `probable`, `conceivable`, `refuted`, `bullshit`);
absence is unspecified and supplies no modal force. Explicit legacy values retain their declared decompositions. `claimModality` carries it on a `StandpointClaim`
and flattens to `standpointModality` on an annotated statement.

### gmeow:StandpointTenure · gmeow:tenureStandpoint · gmeow:tenurePosition · gmeow:standpointClaim

The reified, time-scoped fact that a standpoint held a position over an interval —
recognition granted in 2008 and withdrawn in 2030 is an opened-then-closed tenure,
retained during ordinary revision (Principle 10). Separately authorized erasure follows the [claim lifecycle contract](../../../docs/claim-lifecycle.md). `tenureStandpoint` is the holding standpoint,
`tenurePosition` the standpoint-indexed claim it held, and `standpointClaim`
(functional) the one observation the tenure generates — the tenure being the
time-scoped fact, the claim its observation.

## SSSOM alignments (`mapping-dsl/equivalences/standpoint.ttl`)

All by reference (Principle 5) — GMEOW never imports an external axiom. Compiled to
`mappings/gmeow-standpoint.sssom.tsv`.

| GMEOW | Predicate | Target | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| `gmeow:accordingTo` | `skos:relatedMatch` | `prov:wasAttributedTo` | standpoint ≠ source — a neutral source can record a partisan claim |
| `gmeow:StatementMetadata` | `skos:relatedMatch` | `np:Nanopublication` | a reified statement-with-provenance is a nanopublication minus the PID |
| `gmeow:StatementMetadata` | `skos:relatedMatch` | `crminf:I4_Proposition_Set` | the reified base triple is the proposition a belief is about |
| `gmeow:StandpointTenure` | `skos:relatedMatch` | `crminf:I2_Belief` | a temporal belief held over an interval (CRMinf I2 is itself temporal) |
| `gmeow:standpointModality` | `skos:relatedMatch` | `crminf:J5_holds_to_be` | the belief value (true/probable/possible/false) |
| `gmeow:StatementMetadata` | `skos:relatedMatch` | `wikibase:Statement` | GMEOW's reifier is Wikidata's statement node — but with no rank |
| `gmeow:accordingTo` | `skos:relatedMatch` | `wd:P3680` | Wikidata "statement supported by"; its inverse "disputed by" (P1310) is just another coexisting claim in GMEOW |
| `gmeow:StatementMetadata` | `skos:relatedMatch` | `schema:Claim` | GMEOW refuses ClaimReview's single-verdict false-credibility |
| `gmeow:StatementMetadata` | `skos:relatedMatch` | `prov:Entity` | a reified claim is a provenance entity attributed to its standpoint agent |
| `gmeow:Standpoint` | `skos:relatedMatch` | `prov:Agent` | a standpoint is often (not always) the agent that bears an attribution |
| `gmeow:StatementMetadata` | `skos:relatedMatch` | `oa:Annotation` | body = the quoted triple, target = its subject, creator = the standpoint |
| `gmeow:Standpoint` | `skos:relatedMatch` | `dul:Description` | a standpoint is a DnS Description — a perspective/conceptualization |
| `gmeow:StatementMetadata` | `skos:relatedMatch` | `dul:Situation` | the claim is the DnS Situation that satisfies the standpoint's Description |

**CRMinf parity.** GMEOW is *at least as expressive as* the CIDOC-CRM Argumentation
model: the reified statement is an `I4 Proposition Set`, a `StandpointTenure` is a
temporal `I2 Belief`, the standpoint is the actor of an `I1 Argumentation`, and
`standpointModality` spans `J5 holds to be` (true/probable/possible/**false**) — so a
denial is first-class, not an absence. Carried by the generated CRMinf projection.

**Formal grounding (set comment).** `gmeow:accordingTo` is the axiom-level standpoint
label of **Standpoint Logic** (Standpoint EL, IJCAI 2023; Standpoint-OWL 2,
arXiv:2305.00559; Monodic-S5 over OWL 2 DL, KR 2025); modality = the □/◊ operators
*plus* the CRMinf belief values; `sharpens` = the standpoint ⊆ relation;
`universalStandpoint` = `*`. It also relates to CKR contexts, MVP-OWL multi-viewpoint
classes, and ClaiMaker source-anchored claims (documented in the set comment).

**The Wikidata refusal (set trailer).** GMEOW keeps Wikidata references/qualifiers as
relatedMatch targets but **refuses `wikibase:rank` / `wikibase:PreferredRank`** — a
preferred rank re-creates the single winning slot the edit war is fought over
(Principle 9). The refusal is recorded in the generated TSV, never emitted as a mapping.

## Projections — every standpoint preserved, never a winner

The governing rule is **no winner**: GMEOW ships **no** projection that selects a
single standpoint, because collapsing a contested fact to a chosen frame re-creates
the single winning slot the facility abolishes (Principle 9). All five projections
below preserve *every* standpoint. Two are **lossless** (Standpoint-OWL 2, CRMinf);
the PROV-O, Web Annotation, and schema.org ones are perspective-preserving but lossy
on the belief value (a documented drop, **not** a winner-pick — every standpoint still
appears). (`tests/test_standpoint.py` asserts no frame-selecting executor exists.)

### Standpoint-OWL 2 → `queries/projections/standpoint-owl2.rq`

Re-expresses every reified statement's `accordingTo` + `standpointModality` as the
**`standpointLabel`** encoding consumed by the reference translator
([`cl-tud/standpoint-owl2`](the Standpoint OWL2 implementation) → an off-the-shelf OWL 2 DL reasoner):
`Box` for settled (□), `Diamond` for possible/probable (◊), the standpoint IRI as the
`Standpoint name` (`*` for the universal standpoint). The property IRI is
`…/gmeow#standpointLabel` — the tool matches any property ending in `#standpointLabel`.
Because this encoding labels an *asserted* axiom, a `refuted` (denied) claim cannot be
represented here without asserting what the standpoint rejects, so denials are excluded
from this projection and carried by the CRMinf one below. A standpoint-aware reasoner
runs **per-standpoint and cross-standpoint** consistency over the full graph.

### CRMinf (CIDOC-CRM Argumentation) → `queries/projections/standpoint-crminf.rq`

Re-expresses every reified statement as an `I1 Argumentation` (`P14 carried out by`
the standpoint) that `J2 concluded that` an `I2 Belief` `J4 that` an
`I4 Proposition Set`, which `J5 holds to be` a belief value derived from
`standpointModality` — `true` / `probable` / `possible` / **`false`**. Because the
belief value is explicit, a **denial** (`refuted`) is carried faithfully and the
proposition is *referred to* (`P67 refers to`), never asserted as a fact. This is the
projection that makes GMEOW's *at-least-as-expressive-as-CRMinf* claim concrete.

### Web Annotation → `queries/projections/standpoint-oa.rq`

Re-expresses every reified statement as an `oa:Annotation`: `oa:hasBody` the
(still-reified, never-asserted) quoted statement, `oa:hasTarget` its subject,
`dcterms:creator` the standpoint, `oa:motivatedBy oa:describing`. Perspective-
preserving (every standpoint becomes an annotation by its frame); lossy on the belief
value and confidence, like the PROV-O projection.

### schema.org Claim → `queries/projections/standpoint-schema.rq`

Re-expresses every **non-denied** reified statement as a `schema:Claim` whose
`schema:author` is the standpoint and whose `schema:text` is the rendered proposition
(never the asserted base triple). Per-standpoint claims coexist — there is no single
`schema:ClaimReview` verdict, the false-credibility GMEOW refuses. A `refuted` (denied)
claim is **excluded** (a `schema:author` would misread denial as authorship) and
carried by the CRMinf projection. For the web / fact-check ecosystem.

### PROV-O → `queries/projections/standpoint-prov.rq`

Re-expresses every reified statement as a `prov:Entity` (proposition kept reified,
never asserted) `prov:wasAttributedTo` its standpoint agent, with a
`prov:qualifiedAttribution` node naming the `prov:agent`. Every standpoint is
retained and none is privileged. It is the *provenance* lens: perspective-preserving
but **lossy** on the belief value (`standpointModality`) and `confidence` — those are
carried by the CRMinf and Standpoint-OWL 2 projections. Lossy-drop note (in the
generated header): *belief value (modality) and confidence dropped; attribution
preserved*. This is loss without winner-picking — every standpoint still appears.
